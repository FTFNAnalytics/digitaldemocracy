The national legal sequence is a named gate: 1993 legislation introduced the earlier mixed regime; the 2005 legislation used a different list/majority-bonus regime; the 2017 legislation supplies the mixed framework applicable to the 2022 returns; the 2020 constitutional reform reduced elected chamber sizes. The retained institutional dossier and Camera explanation describe these changes. The 2022 system uses single-member plurality and proportional list components, plus separate overseas arrangements. A single mixed ballot links candidate and list effects; comparing these figures as independent first and second votes would be wrong. Constituency exceptions, thresholds, allocation levels and Constitutional Court decisions must be checked for the particular vintage. No historical result is recomputed using a later chamber size.

Under ordinary municipal rules, small- and larger-municipality mayoral elections differ, with the larger regime using an absolute-majority condition and a runoff when legally required. TAA, Valle d’Aosta, FVG and Sicily have special provisions; the ordinary 15,000-population rule must not be silently applied everywhere. Joint votes, single-candidate quorums, split voting, council bonuses and runoffs require the source-era local statute. This pack records only published figures and explicitly identified rounds. The public 2026 regional material is evidence of current modes, not authority to fabricate past rules.

Law 56/2014 is a current-versus-historical gate, not a deletion instruction for old popular provincial elections. Those earlier contests can be added from official returns with historical legal context. The current FVG legislation is also a transition gate: a statutory provision for direct elections is not proof that a first election has occurred.
